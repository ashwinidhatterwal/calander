# Discover content and selection

The offline editorial collection contains 89 bilingual records: 79 cultural, devotional, narrative and teaching readings, and 10 dated historical records. There is content for every Hindu month; this is a finite curated collection and readings rotate.

Priority: actual festival IDs from the shared location-aware FestivalEngine; sunrise tithi within the matching Purnimanta Hindu month; matching month; daily general rotation. The first two cards follow the priority order, with an additional rotating general reading. Read more reveals the remaining collection without duplicate IDs. Historical anniversaries are shown separately on their matching Gregorian dates. A tithi-based reading is labelled as a reading, not a declaration that a festival or fast must be observed that day. Annual tithi matching is suppressed for an adhik month.

The source is linked on every reading. Content is original short Hindi/English paraphrase, not copied articles. Sacred narratives and theological claims are identified as traditions. Regional practices differ; source links allow readers to explore the original account. The app does not download content or poll a network feed.

## Source coverage

- Drik Panchang: individual Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartik, Margashirsha, Paush, Magha and Phalguna festival pages; Navratri, Pradosh, Satyanarayana and Ekadashi explanations.
- BAPS: Holi and the Prahlad narrative.
- Belur Math: Durga Puja traditions and Sri Ramakrishna on prayer.
- Bhagavad Gita chapters 6, 12 and 17, using Swami Mukundananda’s presentation: short reflections with chapter and verse references in the card text.
- Existing UNESCO and institutional records for Indian cultural heritage and dated history.

## Day details

A civil-day timeline covers local midnight to the following midnight. Each interval includes its paksha and tithi, precise start and end, and a sunrise marker. A final interval clipped by midnight explicitly states whether the tithi continues and its actual ending. This avoids confusing a civil-date boundary with a lunar transition. Advanced Panchang, traditional time windows and eras are expandable. Sun/moon times use 12-hour notation and Hindi AM/PM labels.
