# Discover content and selection

The offline editorial collection contains 137 bilingual records: 127 cultural, devotional, narrative and teaching readings, and 10 dated historical records. There is content for every Hindu month; this is a finite curated collection and readings rotate.

The current Discover UI has exactly three daily sections, each with one reading:

1. Spirituality and good practices: selects from spiritual/traditional readings, preferring the day’s actual observance or matching tithi, otherwise a cycle of specific spiritual teachings, and includes a short practice prompt.
2. Morality and philosophy: rotates a reflection from the new named teachers alongside the existing Gita readings and adds a question to consider in daily life.
3. Today’s facts: shows a matching-date historical anniversary when available, otherwise a rotating cultural or heritage fact. General facts are explicitly labelled as daily discoveries rather than anniversary claims.

These pools are separate, so the same reading cannot occupy multiple sections. There is no expanding data feed or Read more list. The 48 new readings are spread across teacher groups so every entry is reachable, rather than buried beneath month-level summaries. Every new card includes the teacher/tradition, exact reference, paraphrase/story label and its own editorial prompt. Philosophy and non-anniversary facts rotate daily. The general selection API still supports month context for other consumers.

The source is linked on every reading. Content is original short Hindi/English paraphrase, not copied articles. Sacred narratives and theological claims are identified as traditions. Regional practices differ; source links allow readers to explore the original account. The app does not download content or poll a network feed.

## Source coverage

- Drik Panchang: individual Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartik, Margashirsha, Paush, Magha and Phalguna festival pages; Navratri, Pradosh, Satyanarayana and Ekadashi explanations.
- BAPS: Holi and the Prahlad narrative.
- Belur Math: Durga Puja traditions and Sri Ramakrishna on prayer.
- Bhagavad Gita chapters 6, 12 and 17, using Swami Mukundananda’s presentation: short reflections with chapter and verse references in the card text.
- Existing UNESCO and institutional records for Indian cultural heritage and dated history.

Additional sources: Arthashastra (Shamasastry); Katha, Chandogya, Taittiriya, Brihadaranyaka and Mundaka Upanishads; Mahabharata Udyoga Parva (Ganguli); Tirukkural via Project Madurai; Yoga Sutras (Rama Prasada); One Hundred Poems by Kabir (Tagore); Vivekananda’s Complete Works, Karma Yoga; Dhammapada (Buddharakkhita); Tattvartha Sutra (Vijay K. Jain). See the content inventory for individual passage references and links.

## Day details

A civil-day timeline covers local midnight to the following midnight. Each interval includes its paksha and tithi, precise start and end, and a sunrise marker. A final interval clipped by midnight explicitly states whether the tithi continues and its actual ending. This avoids confusing a civil-date boundary with a lunar transition. Advanced Panchang, traditional time windows and eras are expandable. Sun/moon times use 12-hour notation and Hindi AM/PM labels.
